What changed

Being findable now means being describable

The question is no longer whether your brand appears in a list. It is whether a machine reading everything you have ever published can tell who you are without contradicting itself.

Discovery moved to a synthesis layer

People increasingly ask a model rather than scanning a page of results. The answer they get is assembled, not retrieved, which means your brand has to survive being summarised by something that has never spoken to you.

The model builds you out of fragments

It reads your site, your regional variants, your partner and directory listings, your press coverage and copy your agency wrote two years ago. It does not know which of those is most relevant, and nothing on the page tells it.

Contradiction is what gets you dropped

When a brand is described differently across platforms, the model has no stable identity to work from. Brands that do not appear consistently are easier to omit, easier to swap for a competitor, and easier to misrepresent. Inconsistency becomes a legibility problem.

Three ways it goes wrong

  • Skipped: the model has no confident read on you, so it names someone else
  • Described wrongly: it picks the version that appears most often, which may be the old one
  • Attributed wrongly: it fills a gap with something that belongs to a competitor

All three have one cause

Your brand does not say the same thing in every place it appears.

The crux

This is brand maintenance, not messaging

Models do not invent brand descriptions out of nothing. They work from what they find, measured by how often and how consistently they find it. That makes every inconsistency you publish a vote for a different version of you, and it makes coherence across your own properties the input you actually own. Nobody can promise you a place in a model's answer, but you can remove the reasons a model would get you wrong.
